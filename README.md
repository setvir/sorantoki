# Sorantoki (lit. sun speak)

Sorantoki is a deliberately minimal, open-source International Auxiliary Language (IAL) designed to be simple, clear, and kind to learners. It is not a static artifact or a bureaucratic institution; it is a living, open tool for global communication.

## What makes Sorantoki different

Most constructed languages focus solely on grammar and vocabulary, often trapping themselves in rigid academies. Sorantoki focuses equally on accessibility, inclusivity, and organic growth. 

Sorantoki combines three elements that are rarely designed together:

1. **Structural simplicity with predictable constraints**
   - No inflection or irregular forms.
   - Fixed word order and a logical six-slot serial verb template.
   - A small phoneme inventory with no consonant clusters.

2. **Inclusive design as an engineering constraint**
   - Multiple pronunciation variants are equally correct (e.g., *wasa*, *vasa*, *watha*, *vatha*).
   - Single stress rule, no lexical stress exceptions.
   - Optional clarity markers instead of mandatory, exhausting precision.
   - A cultural repair word (*wasa*) that normalizes misunderstanding and removes blame.

3. **Open and Organic Evolution**
   - **No central academy or governance board.** 
   - The language is released "as is" under permissive licenses, allowing the community to use, adapt, and grow it organically based on real-world needs.
   - Evolution is driven by usage and community tools, not by committee votes.

## Design goals (and non-goals)

**Sorantoki aims to:**
- Minimize cognitive load for learners.
- Reduce barriers caused by accent, speech variation, or working-memory demands.
- Encourage calm, cooperative communication.
- Grow vocabulary only when communicative need is demonstrated.
- Remain inspectable, open, and free.

**Sorantoki does not aim to:**
- Maximize expressive density.
- Replace natural languages.
- Compete ideologically with existing auxlangs.
- Enforce a single "correct" accent or register.

## Inclusivity by design

Sorantoki does not claim universal accessibility. Instead, it deliberately reduces known sources of linguistic friction that disproportionately affect some speakers, including:
- Phonological precision requirements.
- Memorization of inflectional paradigms.
- Irregular stress and pronunciation rules.
- Social stigma around "incorrect" speech.

These choices are documented openly so they can be evaluated, challenged, and improved by the community.

## Open Source & Organic Growth

Sorantoki treats language as a shared public utility. Because there is no central language academy, we rely on the "Open Source" model of language evolution:

- **The Canonical Core:** The grammar and core lexicon in this repository are stable, logical, and free of arbitrary exceptions. They serve as the standard reference.
- **Community Extensions:** You are highly encouraged to fork the project, expand the lexicon, or create specialized dialects (e.g., technical jargon, regional variations, or hobbyist vocabularies). 
- **Evolution by Usage:** If the community organically adopts a new compound or derivation that improves communication, it can be merged into the official lexicon in future updates. Usage drives the dictionary, not a board.

- Core grammar and lexicon established and fully open-sourced.
- Community growth is organic, welcoming, and tool-driven.

## How to get involved

At this stage, the most valuable contributions are:
- **Learning and speaking:** Use the language, make mistakes, and use *wasa*!
- **Building tools:** Create apps, flashcards, keyboards, and learning materials (the language itself is CC0!).
- **Creating media:** Write short example texts, stories, or record videos in Sorantoki.
- **Feedback:** [Log points of communicative friction](https://github.com/setvir/sorantoki/discussions/categories/friction-reports) or suggest improvements via GitHub Discussions.
- **Forking:** Create specialized dialects or extensions for your specific community or use-case.

## Your First Word

**wasa**  
*pronounced wasa, vasa, watha, or vatha* — means "I didn't catch that" or "The message wasn't received clearly." It can refer to unclear hearing, unclear speech, or simply unclear understanding.

*wasa* is a gentle, non-blaming way to ask someone to repeat, slow down, or clarify. It signals cooperation, not error. In Sorantoki culture, communication is shared responsibility: if something isn't understood, the listener uses *wasa* without hesitation, and the speaker repeats without feeling corrected.
